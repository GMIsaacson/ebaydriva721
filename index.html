A day in support

What support can include

Support can be understood through the ordinary moments that make up a person’s day—at home, in routines, and out in the community.

Daily routines

Practical support around the rhythms, preferences, and needs of everyday life.

Safety and personal care

Assistance that takes care and safety seriously while respecting the person.

Independent-living skills

Support for building and practicing skills that matter to daily living.

Community participation

Room for activities, connections, and participation beyond the home.

02 / How support happens

A conversation, not a gate.

The path below explains how to begin thinking together about support. It is not a mandatory intake, referral, eligibility, or approval process.

Routines and preferences

Start with the person’s daily life, what matters to them, and the practical support they want to discuss.

Appropriate support

Talk through needs, safety, skills, personal care, and the services that may be appropriate to explore.

Ongoing conversation

Questions can include caregiver employment, screening, training, and supervisor home visits, with details confirmed directly.

Community life

Consider ordinary participation, recreation, outings, and connection alongside support at home.
